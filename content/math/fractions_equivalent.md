## Reading

Two fractions are equivalent when they name the same amount even if the pieces look different. You can build an equivalent fraction by multiplying or dividing the numerator and denominator by the same nonzero whole number. Canceling a common factor shrinks both parts together. Drawing equal shaded regions helps you see why 1/2 and 2/4 match. Equivalent fractions are not a new kind of number; they are different writings of the same quantity. A common mistake is changing only the top or only the bottom. Another is treating cross products as optional. When you compare or add later, rewriting with a shared denominator often starts from equivalence. Keep practicing by predicting the next state before you check. Write one clear sentence of intent, then compare it to the result. That habit matters more than rushing through every example on the first sitting. Say the idea out loud once before you move to the next item so the model stays audible.

## Worked example

1/2 equals 2/4 because both shade half of the same whole. Multiply top and bottom of 1/2 by 2 to get 2/4.
