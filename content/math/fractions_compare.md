## Reading

Comparing fractions asks which amount is larger. With the same denominator, the larger numerator wins. With different denominators, rewrite them as equivalent fractions that share a denominator, then compare numerators. Number lines and shaded diagrams make the comparison visible. Cross-multiplying can also decide order for positive fractions: compare a/b and c/d by checking a*d versus c*b. A frequent mistake is comparing numerators alone when denominators differ. Another is assuming a larger denominator always means a larger fraction. Keep the whole the same while you compare. Keep practicing by predicting the next state before you check. Write one clear sentence of intent, then compare it to the result. That habit matters more than rushing through every example on the first sitting. Say the idea out loud once before you move to the next item so the model stays audible. If a step surprises you, rewrite the same idea with different numbers and try again.

## Worked example

Compare 1/2 and 1/3. Shared denominator 6 gives 3/6 and 2/6, so 1/2 is larger. Cross products 1*3=3 and 1*2=2 agree.
