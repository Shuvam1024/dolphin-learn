## Reading

When two fractions share a denominator, you add the numerators and keep that denominator. 1/5 + 2/5 becomes 3/5 because you are combining same-sized fifths. The denominator still names the size of each part; only the count of parts changes. If the denominators differ, you cannot add the numerators until the parts are the same size — that case is out of scope for this short lesson. For same-denominator sums, rewrite the problem in words first so the kept denominator stays meaningful.

## Worked example

To add `1/5 + 2/5`, keep the shared denominator 5 and add the numerators: `1 + 2 = 3`, so the sum is `3/5`. The parts are the same size, which is why the denominators stay put. If the denominators differed, you would rewrite to equal parts first; with equal denominators the numerators alone combine.
